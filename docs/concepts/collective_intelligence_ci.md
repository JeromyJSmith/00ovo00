# Collective Intelligence & Palace OS Principles

## [CANONICAL] Core Principles of Palace OS Collective Intelligence

### The Great Game
Palace OS's flagship collective intelligence protocol. Participants engage in **"The Great Game"** — a structured game-theoretic interaction where:
- Contradictions are fuel, not failures
- Bad-faith actors receive zero computational influence
- Collaborative decomposition produces win-win outcomes

### Parley
A two-player co-intelligence game where paired participants must navigate contradiction to reach synthesis.

### GRAIL
The **Global Resolution, Alignment, and Inquiry Library** — a dynamic library/memory layer where conflicts are processed into collective intelligence using AI agents.

### Co-Intelligence
The fundamental building block: **"all intelligence is co-intelligence."** No intelligence exists in isolation; it always arises from interaction between distinct entities.

## [CANONICAL] Ternary Logic in Collective Intelligence

### Eliminating the Binary Trap
Traditional CI systems rely on binary choices (yes/no, like/dislike) which:
- Force premature commitments
- Collapse opinion diversity
- Drive populations into groupthink
- Trigger the "principle of explosion" when contradictions arise

### State-2 as an "Epistemic Firewall"
Paraconsistent ternary logic introduces State-2 (Hold) to:
- **Isolate contradictions** — prevent propagation through the system
- **Signal exploration** — treat disagreement as a data point, not a failure
- **Buffer against forced choice** — provide a formal "exit ramp" from binary decisions

## [CANONICAL] Powering Consensus Compositional Game Theory

Ternary logic acts as the computational engine for CCGT:

### Neutralizing Bad Faith
Agents who rigidly force **"binary closure"** — demanding an absolute 0 or 1 and refusing to acknowledge complexity — are granted **zero influence** by the algorithm.

### Rewarding Collaboration
Agents who willingly step into the uncertainty of State-2 and acknowledge contradiction are rewarded with **"edit permissions"** — the power to shape the shared consensus narrative.

### Decomposition for Consensus
Instead of binary stalemates, the system uses State-2 to **decompose** grand debates into specific contexts — identifying exactly where a concept is true, false, and unknown.

## Three Ways of Knowing

### 1. Cognitive Knowing (The Brain)
- **Nature**: Impersonal, objective, abstracted from specific contexts
- **Expression**: Language, symbols, concepts, theories, maps, equations
- **Mechanism**: Neocortex for logical/analytical thinking + memory for storage

### 2. Embodied Knowing (The Senses)
- **Nature**: Deeply personal, subjective, context-specific
- **Expression**: Feeling, sensing, intuiting, imagining
- **Mechanism**: The body as antenna. Includes intuition / "abductive" reasoning — insights that come when we loosen the brain's control

### 3. Enacted Knowing (The Action)
- **Nature**: Tacit "know-how" locked within physical actions and lived experience
- **Expression**: Disclosed through "reflective practice" — consciously thinking about what we are doing
- **Mechanism**: Associative and metaphorical thinking. Integrates both cognitive and embodied data
