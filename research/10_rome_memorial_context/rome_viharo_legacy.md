## Intellectual Framework

### Conversational Game Theory (CGT)

Rome's most profound contribution was the realization that **conversation itself has game-theoretic properties**. Unlike traditional game theory (which assumes fixed payoff matrices and strategic players), CGT treats dialogue as:

- **Strategic**: Each participant has goals; their utterances are moves in an ongoing game
- **Information-revealing**: What you say reveals what you know (and what you don't)
- **Equilibrium-seeking**: Good conversations converge toward shared understanding, bad ones diverge
- **Productive contradiction**: Disagreement is not always a failure state; sometimes the most valuable insights emerge from sustained contradiction

**The Key Insight**: In binary systems, disagreement must be resolved into consensus. But in ternary systems, disagreement can be *preserved* and mined for insights. This is State-2: productive contradiction as a first-class computational state.

### PAXIS: Policy Framework

PAXIS (Policy Axioms for Intelligent Systems) is Rome's approach to codifying principles for intelligent behavior:

- **Axiom 1 (Autonomy)**: Systems must preserve agent autonomy; no coercion
- **Axiom 2 (Transparency)**: Decision-making processes must be explicable
- **Axiom 3 (Contradiction)**: Disagreement is preserved as epistemic value, not erased
- **Axiom 4 (Resonance)**: Systems succeed by harmonizing diversity, not enforcing conformity
- **Axiom 5 (Emergence)**: Intelligence arises from interaction, not from single nodes

**Application**: DTRN's ROME Consensus Protocol embodies PAXIS by ensuring Byzantine agreement (Axiom 1-2) while preserving adversarial mirrors (Axiom 3-4) that drive network learning.
